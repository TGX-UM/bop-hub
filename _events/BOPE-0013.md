This is the beneficial outcome. Mesnage and Buchinger are explicit that a change in a biomarker or risk factor qualifies as a beneficial outcome only when it translates into tangible improvement in how a person feels, functions or survives.

### Evidence at the outcome

A meta-analysis of 21 studies and 1226 participants found fasting decreased liver fat and steatosis independently of participant age, health status, weight status or intervention duration. An 8-week randomised trial of modified alternate-day fasting achieved about 5% weight loss with reductions in liver enzymes and fibrosis markers against a no-diet control. A 12-week trial comparing a 5:2 diet with continuous calorie restriction in 60 patients found similar weight loss but greater liver-specific benefit: moderate-to-severe steatosis fell to 30% in the 5:2 arm versus 59% under daily restriction.

### No ontology process term

`process:`, `object:` and `action:` are empty: this is a disease state rather than a biological process. The disease is recorded on the BOP page in the `diseases:` slot as `MONDO:0013209`, and the phenotype as `HP:0001397`.

### Not every patient reaches it

A BOP does not assert that every patient achieves the beneficial outcome. It maps how the intervention can plausibly lead to that outcome in at least a subset of individuals.

### Crosswalk to AOP-Wiki

`aopwiki_ke` is deliberately **empty**. AOP-Wiki has no event for the resolution of steatosis,
because an AOP has no reason to describe one. What it has is the adverse mirror: key event 459,
"Increase, Liver steatosis", which appears in 22 AOPs. Recording 459 as this event's
`aopwiki_mirror_ke` rather than its `aopwiki_ke` keeps the distinction honest, since the two
share an object but have opposite directions and sit at different levels of biological
organisation.

Nine AOPs currently converge on hepatic steatosis as their adverse outcome, listed in this BOP's
`related_aops`. Those are the pathways this BOP counteracts, and the convergence is the point: a
single beneficial pathway can act against a whole family of adverse ones.
